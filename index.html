INCIDENT FILE EW-31

An Alarm on the 31st Floor

At 10.15 a.m., a fire alarm sounded on the 31st floor of a fictional office building. The fire began in the east wing, close to Mary Lin’s desk.

Everyone evacuated safely. After the fire was extinguished and the building declared safe, investigators collected recordings and object records. Four colleagues had been on the floor that morning, and James’s birthday decorations were being prepared.

Your task: Identify whose action caused the fire, the object involved and whether the incident was deliberate or accidental.

Investigation rules

Exactly one of these four colleagues caused the fire through a recorded action. There were no accomplices or other causes. Decide from the evidence whether the action was deliberate or accidental. The confirmed footage, clock times and fire investigator’s report are accurate.

At 10.14 a.m., each colleague was in a different listed location. Each had brought or owned one different listed object that morning. Ownership does not mean an object was used.

You are reviewing evidence after the emergency. Firefighters and authorised adults handle the scene. A rumour about clumsiness is not evidence of responsibility.

Incident Evidence

Exhibit 01 · The exact time

The fire alarm recorded 10.15 a.m. A synchronised office recording showed the first flames at 10.14 a.m., at the east-wing desk.

Exhibit 02 · Peter’s location

The corridor camera showed Peter entering the toilet at 10.05 a.m. and leaving only when the alarm sounded at 10.15 a.m. It covered the only entrance and exit continuously. He was therefore in the toilet at 10.14 a.m.

Exhibit 03 · James’s location

Continuous printing-room footage showed James working at the jammed printer from 10.08 a.m. until the alarm. He did not leave the room.

Exhibit 04 · Lily’s location

Continuous canteen footage showed Lily seated there from 10.09 a.m. until the alarm. She had left the east wing before the fire began.

Exhibit 05 · Mary’s location

The east-wing camera showed Mary arranging paper decorations at her desk at 10.14 a.m. No one else was at the desk during the relevant interval.

Exhibit 06 · The matchsticks

Matchsticks were found in Peter’s desk cupboard. The morning object register confirmed they belonged to Peter. The investigator found no evidence that they were used in this incident.

Exhibit 07 · The magnifying glass

Lily had brought the magnifying glass to look at insect pictures in a book. It stayed in her closed bag during the incident.

Exhibit 08 · The candle

Mary brought the candle for her vintage-style desk decoration. The register assigned it to her. Each of the four objects belonged to a different colleague; the lighter was the remaining object.

Exhibit 09 · The lighter

The remaining object, James’s birthday lighter, stayed unused in its closed gift box in the printing room. Its ownership does not mean it caused the fire.

Exhibit 10 · What the recording showed

A verified close view showed Mary accidentally bumping the candle holder while reaching for a decoration. Paper decorations came into contact with the lit candle immediately afterwards, and the first flames appeared. She immediately stepped back and raised the alarm. The recording showed no intentional attempt to ignite anything.

Exhibit 11 · The fire-origin report

The fire investigator confirmed the point of origin at the candle beside Mary’s paper decorations. The printer, electrical wiring and other listed objects were ruled out as causes. The report matched the recorded sequence.

Exhibit 12 · The misleading claims

Peter said the cheese he ate had expired and blamed James because of the printing room’s position. Someone described Mary as clumsy. Neither claim establishes the fire’s cause; the timed recordings and origin report do.

Teacher Case Solution

Mary Lin · Candle · East-wing desk · Accidental fire

Peter, James and Lily were continuously recorded elsewhere at 10.14 a.m. Mary was alone at the desk. The verified close view shows her accidental bump followed immediately by paper contacting the candle and the first flames. The fire-origin report independently confirms the candle as the source and excludes other causes.

Her response and the full recorded sequence support an accident. The case does not establish deliberate arson or concealment. Describing her as clumsy would not prove responsibility.

Peter owns the matchsticks, Lily the magnifying glass and Mary the candle. Under the one-object-per-person rule, the remaining lighter belongs to James. Neither ownership nor proximity establishes causation.

Ending: everyone remains safe. Mary explains what happened, and the office reviews its decoration and fire-prevention practices with the responsible adults.
